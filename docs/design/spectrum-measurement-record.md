# Spectrum measurement record

Status: schema-4 activity method retained in schemas 5 and 6. Schema 5 adds waveform records; schema 6 defaults to compact power storage. [Implementation status](../engineering/implementation-status.md) and [validation scope](../engineering/quality-and-validation.md) describe current capabilities and qualification limits.

## Measurement definitions

New acquisition uses contiguous 4096-sample periodic-Hann FFTs with no deliberate sample skipping. Frequency spacing is sample_rate/4096; effective noise bandwidth is 1.5 times that spacing. At 8 MS/s spacing is 1953.125 Hz; at 20 MS/s it is 4882.8125 Hz. Only bins whose complete frequency edges fit within the configured span are retained. Reports expose actual covered edges, which can be slightly narrower than the requested interval.

Per-bin linear power is abs(FFT)^2 / (4096 * sum(window^2)). Summing all FFT bins of a unit complex tone gives 0 dBFS. This is integrated bin power relative to digital full scale, not dBm, field strength, power spectral density or an antenna calibration. A Hann-windowed tone distributes power across neighboring bins. Schema 1-3 used a different measurement normalization and 25-percent time sampling; do not mix their raw level thresholds with schema 4.

Activity means a bin meets or exceeds the saved fixed dBFS/bin threshold in a complete FFT. The retained frame-major joint bitmap preserves simultaneous versus alternating frequency activity. Combining an interval means the union of busy frames across the intersecting recorded bins, never the sum/average of per-bin busy percentages. The reported interval is quantized to actual bin edges. Missing observations do not become quiet measurements. The bitmap retains activity only at the original threshold: a lower threshold cannot be reconstructed later from tile means/peaks, and no post-capture control claims to do so.

Raw any-bin occupancy always retains every selected bin, including the receiver center. Retrospective analysis additionally computes center-only and outside-center busy-time unions from the same FFT activity masks. The center guard selects bins whose centers lie within ±2 recorded bin widths of the nominal receiver center: five aligned bins when the complete guard is selected, or its intersection with the selected bins. Reports expose the actual outer bin edges and selected/unguarded bin counts. Outside-center occupancy uses the same observed-time denominator and is unavailable when no unguarded bin remains. Simultaneous center and outside activity means these durations cannot be obtained by subtracting center busy time from raw busy time.

The optional **Exclude receiver-center region from time/GPS view** comparison defaults on where the selection includes guarded bins. It addresses possible receiver-center contamination without classifying it as an artifact: real signals may also lie there. The excluded frequencies remain unassessed by this comparison, not proven quiet. Raw aggregate values and per-frequency measurements stay visible, including the shaded guard. No threshold, stored mask, power, event or quality flag is changed by this view.

In Detailed mode, approximately 20 ms tiles retain linear-averaged mean powers, per-bin peak powers, complete FFT masks, sample bounds, UTC/elapsed bounds, spectral background estimate, clipped-sample count, quality flags, and optional receiver fixes at both ends. Stored powers are quantized to hundredths of a dB; masks are compressed losslessly with bounded native RLE/raw fallback. No phase, raw samples, payload or ciphertext is stored. Longer legacy five-second aggregates remain convenience summaries; the tiles are authoritative for detailed analysis.

The background estimate is a lower spectral percentile across a tile, not a measured per-bin noise floor or a guarantee of signal-to-noise accuracy. A broad occupied band can contaminate it. It is flagged uncertain and never silently raises the fixed activity threshold. ADC-near-limit components flag clipping; absence of that flag does not prove the analog front end was linear or free of overload. Events near the receiver center are flagged for possible DC artifacts, not automatically discarded.

## Compact power and report support

Compact mode retains the same fine activity masks, grid and time bounds, but shares frame-weighted linear mean powers and per-bin maxima over at most one second of contiguous acquisition. It retains background, clipping and quality per original tile. GPS endpoints reference unchanged original fixes, and duplicate legacy five-second bin arrays are omitted. Readers identify the wider power support with quality flag 512 (`power_time_aggregated`). Power selected within only part of a block, including a geographic subset, must not be interpreted as precisely localized within that block. Activity and its busy/observed-time denominator remain unchanged. The original fixed threshold still cannot be changed retrospectively.

Summary CSV reports are now the ordinary export. Frequency reports retain one row per measured bin; time reports union activity across the chosen frequency interval and default to 60-second groups. Geographic reports retain one row per measured bin and receiver tile-end cell, defaulting to approximately 100-metre cells. They expose unlocated records and cell-crossing attribution without interpolating movement. Fine stored fixes are unchanged. Blank occupancy means unobserved; reported gaps and unknown upstream loss remain separate. Reports preserve method, threshold, gains, offset, actual frequency bounds, observation duration and uncertainty.

Detailed CSV/GeoJSON remains an explicit whole-session archive export. It contains the underlying measurements and can still be very large, especially because CSV repeats binary arrays as hex. Neither report grouping nor a compact recording can reconstruct discarded subsecond mean-power history or undecoded payloads. Ordinary reports do not contain those binary arrays.

## Generic energy events

Adjacent above-threshold frequency bins connect to touching envelopes in consecutive FFT frames. Event records contain observed lower/upper frequency edges, first/end sample and times, linear-mean active-bin power, strongest bin power, observed active duration, GPS endpoints and quality flags. There are at most 64 active tracks. Track overflow groups components with merged/truncated flags. Approximately two-second persistent components split with truncation flags. An event may contain multiple sources or fragments of one source. Counts and widths must not be labeled packets, devices or modem settings.

## Candidate RF bursts (derived method 1)

Schema 5 additionally stores **waveform observations** from transient complex-signal analysis, independently of this energy grouping. Their inferred BW/SF, received center, observed preamble/delimiter times, fit fractions, contributing-subband count and receiver-position association are classification evidence, not energy width, packet airtime or transmitter identity. Overlapping subbands can update one observation ID; ambiguity and range-edge flags remain explicit. Discovery status, subband progress and bounded-queue loss intervals are separate records. Accepted input, processed 2 MS/s subband samples, missing receiver input and result loss are different units/stages and must not be summed into airtime. Primary RF acquisition gaps also constrain discovery. See [the detector method and validation](../engineering/quality-and-validation.md).

The default result list groups activity from complete measurement tiles rather than trying to repair the capped raw-event list. A bin seeds a candidate when it is active in at least two FFT frames within a recorded approximately 20 ms tile. Components bridge at most two empty frequency bins, and tracks can continue over at most 40 ms of measured quiet time. The receiver-center guard is kept separate. A previously established frequency span can absorb separated sweep pieces when they match only that track, stay within its earlier span (plus two bins), and overlap in no more than 25% of their union of active frames. This is flagged ambiguous because it is temporal association, not protocol identification. Simultaneous separated components and components claimed by multiple tracks are not silently merged. Ambiguous predecessors end once, avoiding repeated cascades.

Grouping is bounded to 128 pending tracks and approximately ten-second output segments. Overflow/end/query/grid/gap boundaries are exposed as limited groups. Missing measurement time always breaks a track; short measured quiet gaps may connect it. Width is the outer edge span of supported frequency bins, not a decoded bandwidth, LoRa identity, or 99% occupied-power bandwidth. Threshold, strength, FFT leakage and overlapping emitters can broaden it; weak or very brief activity may remain ungrouped. A sweep need not complete within a tile, so full-bandwidth recovery is not guaranteed.

Duration is the first-to-last assigned activity interval; active duration unions assigned FFT intervals and excludes quiet grouping gaps. Summing candidate durations is not channel occupancy. The original per-frame masks, powers, events, acquisition counters, schemas and exports are unchanged. Single-frame/unsupported activity remains in that evidence and in the raw-fragment detail view. Groups are reconstructed by the same code during live capture and saved queries, with frequency/time/position filters applied to the latter. Full-query group counts remain separate from capped detail rows. Groups are not new persisted/exported packet records.

The live list uses a one-second display snapshot, stable start-time ordering and a pause control independent of acquisition. Raw details are opt-in in the same paced list. Saved groups reflect the selected analysis interval and do not update independently of that query. See [grouping validation](../engineering/quality-and-validation.md).

## Position and coverage

GPS association uses the most recent valid fix at or before the observation, with a two-second age limit for serial GPS. Manual position is explicitly stationary. Saved fixes retain GNSS UTC, host monotonic receipt time, source, optional altitude/HDOP and satellite count. Tile start/end fixes describe the receiver's sampled trajectory; current reporting uses the endpoint association and does not interpolate a transmitter location.

Analysis reports observed durations lacking tile-start and tile-end positions independently, clipped to the query interval. These durations can overlap and must not be added. Endpoint position coverage is observed time minus missing-end time; it measures association availability, not GPS accuracy or continuous knowledge between fixes. Geographic filtering excludes missing endpoints before these totals are calculated. Missing positions do not remove RF observed time from an unfiltered query or imply an acquisition gap. These are derived values; stored flags and schema remain unchanged.

Known application drops and partial FFT tails have sample/time intervals and reasons. A source-stall interval has unknown missing-sample count. HackRF upstream loss remains explicitly unknown. Host clock/sample progression timestamps are not PPS-disciplined hardware timestamps. Interrupted sessions remain incomplete. Flags and exposure denominators accompany results.

## Retrospective reporting

Select arbitrary frequency bounds, elapsed-time bounds and optionally a geographic rectangle. Missing-position records are excluded from geographic queries but remain available without the area filter. Each analysis reads one stable database snapshot, so metadata, recorded extent and measurements agree while acquisition continues. The resolved time selection is clipped to the earliest/latest recorded tile-or-gap bounds, independently of geographic matches.

Time-series/position display covers the complete resolved interval rather than stopping after the first 2000 buckets. Requested bucket width is a minimum: it grows when necessary to fit the observation limit, and the result reports both widths and whether it was coarsened. Buckets are anchored at the resolved start; only observed durations enter their occupancy denominators. Gaps remain missing observations, and aggregate/per-bin values still use all selected measurements. A zero observation limit explicitly disables plotted buckets without disabling totals. Each displayed position remains the last associated receiver fix in its bucket; coarser buckets can span more movement and retain merged/boundary flags. Event rows retain their separate bounded view and truncation notice. All widths are user-selected views; there is no ranking engine.

Channel power means integrate recorded bin powers in linear units. Partial-tile time selection, including display buckets shorter than a tile, uses the tile power mean with a boundary flag; frame masks provide finer busy-time timing. The sum of per-bin maxima is labeled a peak-envelope upper bound because their peaks may occur at different times. Do not label it instantaneous measured channel peak. Event overlaps, truncation and boundary flags remain visible.

The explicit detailed-archive CSV is the reproducible measurement export, including activity runs and method metadata. It retains the original all-bin summaries and masks; the center diagnostic and display buckets add no stored/exported fields. GeoJSON exports located RF summaries directly from recorded tiles, with raw all-bin busy time, independently of display buckets or the center-view switch. It does not export the outside-center comparison as if it described the full band. Coordinates and authorized content remain separately opt-in. These are field-semantics guarantees, not a claim that newly generated exports are byte-identical across software versions. New detailed recording uses more disk than coarse summaries; size depends on range, duration and mask compressibility. Memory buffers and query display are bounded; disk-full/write errors must stop/flag the session rather than silently reduce evidence.

Schema 1-3 sessions are read-only and cannot reconstruct detailed timing, event widths or joint occupancy that was never recorded. No migration invents those observations. Actual RF passband/sensitivity calibration, moving GPS accuracy and other desktop platforms retain separate validation requirements.
